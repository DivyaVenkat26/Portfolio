Manufacturing Downtime and Efficiency Analysis

Excel Project

  1. Home
  2. Manufacturing Downtime and Efficiency Analysis

Introduction:

The Manufacturing Downtime & Efficiency Analysis dashboard provides a detailed overview of production performance by analyzing downtime patterns, operational efficiency, and key factors affecting manufacturing processes. This analysis helps identify critical issues, evaluate operator performance, and understand how downtime impacts overall production efficiency.

Data Preparation:

  1. Data Loading:

    Multiple datasets including production, downtime, and product details were loaded into Power Query for transformation and analysis.

  2. Data Cleaning:

    Handled missing values, corrected errors (such as missing end time), and ensured data consistency across all tables.

  3. Data Type Adjustment:

    Adjusted data types for columns such as date, time, and numeric fields to ensure accurate calculations.

  4. Merge Queries:

    Combined production, downtime, and product datasets using Merge Queries to create a unified dataset for analysis.

  5. Data Transformation:

    Unpivoted downtime attributes to normalize the dataset and enable detailed analysis across different issue types.

  6. Calculated Column:

    Created a new metric Downtime Impact (Downtime × Factor) to measure the severity and business impact of each downtime event.

Pivot Tables:

Pivot tables were created to summarize and analyze key production and downtime metrics:

  1. Downtime Impact by Issue:

    Identifies which issues contribute the most to overall downtime impact.

  2. Downtime by Product:

    Analyzes which products experience higher downtime.

  3. Operator-wise Downtime:

    Evaluates operator performance based on downtime generated.

  4. Production Time vs Downtime:

    Compares productive time against downtime to assess efficiency.

  5. Downtime by Attribute:

    Breaks down downtime into categories such as machine failure, operator delay, and other issues.

Measures/KPIs:

  1. Total Downtime (Hours):

    Total time lost due to downtime across all batches.

  2. Total Production Time (Hours):

    Total available production time.

  3. Production Efficiency (%):

    Calculated as (Production Time - Downtime) / Production Time.

  4. Average Downtime per Batch (mins):

    Average downtime experienced per production batch.

  5. Total Batches:

    Total number of batches processed during the analysis period.

Pivot Charts:

Charts were created to visually represent downtime patterns and efficiency insights:

  1. Top 5 Issue by Downtime Impact (Bar Chart):

    Highlights the most critical issues contributing to production loss.

  2. Downtime Impact by Product (Column Chart):

    Shows which products are most affected by downtime.

  3. Operator-wise Downtime Impact(Bar Chart):

    Compares performance across operators.

  4. Production vs Downtime (Column Chart):

    Displays efficiency by comparing productive time and downtime.

  5. Downtime Impact Trend Over Time (Line Chart):

    Analyzes how downtime varies across different time periods.

  6. Downtime Impact by Flavor (Pie Chart):

    Shows the percentage distribution of downtime across different product flavors, helping identify which flavors contribute most to production delays.

Results:

  1. Downtime by Issue:

    Machine Adjustment recorded the highest downtime impact, followed by Machine Failure and Batch Coding Error, indicating that operational adjustments are the primary contributors to production delays.

  2. Product-wise Downtime:

    CO-600 contributed the highest downtime, with RB-600 as the second highest, highlighting specific products that require process optimization.

  3. Operator-wise Performance:

    Charlie accounted for the highest downtime, followed by Dee, Dennis, and Mac, indicating variation in operator performance and potential areas for training or process standardization.

  4. Flavor-wise Downtime Distribution:

    Cola flavor contributed to 56% of total downtime, followed by Root Beer (19%) and Lemon Lime (12%), showing that certain product variations have a higher impact on production efficiency.
